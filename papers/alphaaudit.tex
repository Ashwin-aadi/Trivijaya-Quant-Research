\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A language model that writes trading strategies is a machine for manufacturing plausible
backtests, and the two failure modes that make those backtests worthless---lookahead bias
and multiple testing---are precisely the two a naive evaluation cannot see. We build the
apparatus that measures them, and we run it end to end on Indian equities.

A local 7B model (\texttt{qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4\_K\_M}, one fixed prompt, temperature
0.8) generated $N=1{,}550$ candidate strategies over a survivorship-free, point-in-time
universe of the 100 most liquid NSE equities, rebuilt quarterly from exchange bhavcopy
archives using only prior sessions (185 distinct constituents across 20 rebalances; 41 of
the first cohort had left by the last). Each
candidate was executed by a point-in-time backtester that raises rather than warns on a
stale signal, and priced by a transaction-cost model built for the Indian statutory
regime---STT, exchange and IPFT charges, SEBI turnover fee, stamp duty, GST, and a
per-scrip depository charge---applied at the rates in force on each trade's own date,
with volume-participation slippage and square-root impact. Three independently ablatable
auditors judge each candidate: an AST-based leakage detector, a statistical layer
computing the Deflated Sharpe Ratio against a hash-chained, append-only trial ledger the
generator cannot write to, and a local-LLM semantic layer reading each stated rationale
against its own implementation ($\kappa = 0.589$ against 50 human labels).

\keyfinding{\textbf{The dominant failure mode of an LLM strategy researcher is not that it
cheats---it is that it does not run.} All 1,550 draws returned parseable,
interface-conforming code; 631 (40.7\%) survived contact with real data; 406 of those
(64.3\%) never took a position. \textbf{14.5\% of the corpus produced a strategy that both
executes and trades.} The auditor is rarely the binding constraint.}

\noindent
Net of costs, the best of 225 rankable strategies falls from a gross Sharpe of 1.3049 to
1.2807---and it survives only because it barely trades. The median strategy loses 0.194 of
Sharpe and 4.4\% of annual return; the mean loses 0.587 and 58.1\%, the gap between the two
being a tail of candidates churning at over 80$\times$ annual turnover. \textbf{35 of 225
(15.6\%) reverse the sign of their Sharpe once costs are charged, and 11 are bankrupted
outright.} None reverse in the opposite direction, as none can. Two of eleven standard
academic factors also flip from profitable to unprofitable, and momentum, at a net Sharpe
of 0.966, no longer beats equal-weighting the same universe. Against an honest trial count
of $N=1{,}887$, \textbf{zero of 631 evaluated candidates attain a deflated Sharpe
probability above 0.95}; the corpus maximum is $4.4\times10^{-8}$.

The abstention experiment returns a null. Sweeping coverage from 1.0 to 0.05 over all seven
layer combinations and scoring on a holdout that was evaluated exactly twice under logged
authorisation and is now permanently closed, \textbf{no configuration of the auditor beats
random rejection at matched coverage} (holdout AUAP $-1.14$ to $-1.43$ against a random
95\% interval of $[-1.22, -0.86]$; two configurations fall below it). The single
configuration that beat random in sample was the one that provably cannot---its ranking
signal is a monotone function of the quantity it was scored against---and out of sample it
fails with the others. We report this null rather than tune past it, and note that it
survived a subsequent correction of the entire cost implementation: the magnitudes moved,
the conclusion did not. The null is confounded with corpus degeneracy and cannot separate
\textit{``the auditor is uninformative''} from \textit{``this corpus is too weak to test
it''}; both explanations survive the data, and that caveat travels with the number
permanently.

We release the leaky and clean fixture suites, the audited 1,550-candidate corpus, the
Indian cost model, and a frozen one-shot benchmark protocol, so that the second claim can
be contested with a stronger generator than ours.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Ask a large language model for a systematic equity strategy and it will give you one. It
will give you three hundred. Each will arrive with a plausible economic rationale, clean
code, and---if you backtest it naively---a Sharpe ratio that would embarrass most
professional managers. This is not evidence of skill. It is evidence that the search space
is large and that a backtest is a very weak instrument.

Two mechanisms account for nearly all of it. The first is \emph{leakage}: the code sees
information it could not have had. A signal computed from today's close that trades at
today's close is not a strategy, it is a memory. The second is \emph{multiple testing}: if
$N$ independent strategies are tried and the best is selected, the maximum of $N$ draws
from a mean-zero distribution is positive by construction, and grows with $\log N$
\citep{harvey2016,bailey2014}. Neither mechanism announces itself. Both produce equity
curves that look exactly like skill.

An LLM strategy generator makes both worse, and it does so in a specific and
under-appreciated way. It does not merely raise $N$; it raises $N$ silently. A researcher
who tries twenty ideas by hand remembers trying twenty. A generator that emits a candidate
every few seconds, unattended, destroys the researcher's ability to know their own
denominator---and the Deflated Sharpe Ratio is meaningless without an honest one.

This paper builds the instrument, not the strategy. We construct an auditor $A$ that maps
a candidate strategy to a rejection decision, a reason, and a deflated performance
estimate, with three independently ablatable layers: an AST-based static analyser, a
statistical deflation layer backed by a tamper-evident trial ledger, and a local-LLM
semantic layer that reads each strategy's stated rationale against its own implementation.
We then ask whether the auditor is worth having, by measuring \emph{selective deployment}:
if you act only on the fraction $c$ of candidates the auditor most trusts, does realised
out-of-sample performance improve as $c$ falls? The area under that curve, $\mathrm{AUAP}$,
is our primary metric, and the null hypothesis---that $A$ is no better than rejecting at
random---is something we test rather than assume away.

We run the whole apparatus on Indian equities, a market chosen for a reason that turns out
to matter more than expected. Indian statutory transaction costs are heavy, asymmetric
between buy and sell legs, and include a per-scrip depository charge that does not scale
with trade size. A cost model that treats them as a flat basis-point haircut is not a
simplification; it is a different experiment.

\subsection*{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.2em}
\item \textbf{A negative result about LLM strategy generation that is not about leakage.}
  Of 1,550 candidates, all 1,550 parsed and conformed to the engine interface; 631 executed
  against real data; 406 of those never traded. The auditor is not the binding
  constraint---the interpreter is (\S\ref{sec:corpus}).
\item \textbf{A time-varying Indian transaction cost model}, verified line by line against a
  discount broker's public calculator, wired \emph{inside} the backtest engine and charged
  per leg. It reverses the sign of 15.6\% of profitable candidates and bankrupts eleven
  (\S\ref{sec:costs-results}).
\item \textbf{An honest trial counter}, hash-chained and append-only, outside the
  generator's write path, and the demonstration that at $N=1{,}887$ nothing in the corpus
  survives deflation (\S\ref{sec:deflation}).
\item \textbf{A pre-registered null.} The auditor does not beat random rejection at matched
  coverage, in any of seven layer configurations, on a holdout evaluated under logged
  authorisation and now permanently closed. We report it, and we report that it survived a
  full correction of our own cost implementation (\S\ref{sec:negative}).
\item \textbf{A frozen benchmark}: leaky and clean fixture suites, the audited corpus, and a
  one-shot evaluation protocol (\S\ref{sec:release}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Backtest overfitting.} That the reported Sharpe of a selected strategy is
biased upward is old news; quantifying the bias is not. \citet{bailey2014} derive the
Deflated Sharpe Ratio, which adjusts an observed Sharpe for trial count, sample length,
skewness and kurtosis by raising the threshold a strategy must clear rather than lowering
its estimate. \citet{bailey2017} introduce the Probability of Backtest Overfitting via
combinatorially symmetric cross-validation. \citet{harvey2016} argue that the conventional
$t>2$ hurdle should be roughly $t>3$ once the published factor zoo is treated as one
enormous multiple-testing exercise. \citet{lopezdeprado2018} supplies the machinery we use
for purged $k$-fold cross-validation with embargo. Our contribution is not to these
statistics but to their \emph{plumbing}: all of them require $N$, and none of the papers
that define them address what happens when the researcher is a program that cannot
remember how many times it has tried.

\paragraph{Transaction costs and the anomaly literature.} \citet{novymarx2016} show that a
large share of published anomalies do not survive realistic US trading costs, and that the
survivors are disproportionately the low-turnover ones. \citet{frazzini2018} estimate
real-world costs from live institutional trading and find them lower than the academic
consensus for large-cap strategies. Both literatures are calibrated to US market
microstructure. India's cost structure differs qualitatively---Securities Transaction Tax
on both legs, stamp duty on the buy alone, a flat depository fee on the sell---and to our
knowledge no open Indian equivalent exists.

\paragraph{Selective prediction.} The idea that a model should be allowed to abstain is
well developed in classification \citep{elyaniv2010,geifman2017}, where risk--coverage
curves are standard. We import the framework and change what it measures: coverage is the
fraction of \emph{strategies deployed}, and risk is realised out-of-sample Sharpe. The
translation is not free, and \S\ref{sec:design} discusses where it strains.

\paragraph{LLMs as code and research agents.} Code-generating models are now reliable
enough to produce syntactically valid, interface-conforming programs at scale
\citep{chen2021}, and finance-specific models exist \citep{wu2023}. What is not established
is whether the output constitutes research. Our corpus statistics
(\S\ref{sec:corpus}) are a direct measurement of that gap for one 7B open-weights model.

\section{Infrastructure}
\label{sec:infra}

Three components had to be built before any experiment could be run. Each is boring, and
each is load-bearing in the sense that a defect in it would silently invalidate everything
downstream. We describe them at the level of detail required to know whether to trust them.

\subsection{A survivorship-free, point-in-time universe}

The universe on date $t$ must contain exactly the securities investable on date $t$,
including those that later delisted. A universe built from today's index membership and
applied backwards embeds survivorship bias so thoroughly that no downstream correction can
remove it.

We did not use a published index membership list, because free point-in-time NIFTY~100
constituent history could not be sourced. Instead the universe is \emph{rules-based}: at
each quarterly rebalance, the 100 NSE equities with the highest trailing 126-session median
traded value, computed using only sessions strictly before the rebalance date, with a
buffer band to suppress rank churn. Nothing consults a membership list, so a company that
collapses simply stops qualifying rather than vanishing from history.

The 41 names that were in the first universe and absent from the last are the direct

Prices come from NSE's own end-of-day bhavcopy archives, adjusted for splits and bonuses.
We cross-checked against \texttt{yfinance} and found a \textbf{17.47\% disagreement rate on
adjusted closes} across 35,757 aligned observations. Inspection of the worst offenders
showed \emph{exact} integer ratios---BAJFINANCE at precisely $10\times$, ASHOKLEY at
precisely $2\times$---for securities with no declared corporate action in the window.
Genuine data noise is never exactly $10\times$. We designate the exchange file
authoritative and report the discrepancy rather than reconciling it, because the
reconciliation would be a fiction.

\subsection{An engine that makes lookahead hard to express}

Point-in-time discipline is enforced structurally rather than by convention. A strategy is
never handed the price panel; it receives a \texttt{MarketView} already truncated to before
its decision moment. Every signal carries an \texttt{information\_available\_at} stamp, and
the engine raises \texttt{PointInTimeError}---an exception, not a warning---if that stamp is
not strictly prior to the fill session. A signal formed at session $t$'s close fills at
session $t{+}1$'s \emph{open}.

Returns are accrued by provenance: a position carried from the previous session earns the
full close-to-close move including the overnight gap, while only the portion bought this
morning earns from the open. Collapsing both into open-to-close would silently liquidate
the book every evening, discarding most of the equity premium in the sample and making
every long-horizon strategy look structurally unprofitable.

\paragraph{Deliberate self-sabotage.} Three strategies that cheat on purpose are permanent
fixtures: one uses next-day return directly, one fits a \texttt{StandardScaler} on the full
sample before splitting, one selects its universe from end-of-period membership. Gross of
costs they return Sharpe ratios of \textbf{12{,}098}, \textbf{2.33} and \textbf{1.23}
respectively. Each holds its number when known data artifacts are removed, confirming it
cheats for its intended reason rather than riding a data scar. Thirty honest strategies
form the false-positive control. \emph{We built the disease before building the test for
it.}

As a sanity check, equal-weighting the universe returns 19.98\% annualised at 21.14\%
volatility over 1,232 sessions, with 7.64\% annualised tracking error and 0.9334
correlation against the NIFTY~100 price index---close enough to be a market proxy, distant
enough to reflect that it is an equal-weighted liquidity portfolio rather than a
capitalisation-weighted index.

\subsection{The Indian transaction cost model}
\label{sec:costmodel}

Costs are charged \emph{inside} the engine, per name and per side, at the statutory rates in
force on each trade's own date. The two legs are not symmetric---stamp duty falls on the buy
alone, the depository charge on the sell alone---so a blended round-trip rate gets both
wrong. Rates are stored as a schedule of complete epoch snapshots (three, reflecting the
April 2024 NSE reduction and the October 2024 SEBI true-to-label change) rather than as
deltas, so no edit can silently corrupt a neighbouring period.

Table~\ref{tab:cost} is pinned as a unit test, asserted to the paisa, so no future rate edit
can move it silently. Slippage is modelled as linear in volume participation and impact as a

one. \S\ref{sec:costs-results} reports what fraction of measured drag they are responsible
for, which is the only honest way to bound the damage they can do.

\paragraph{The depository charge is not scale-invariant.} At \rupee15.34 per scrip per sell
instruction, a hundred-name book rebalancing once costs \rupee1,534 regardless of position
size---15 basis points of a \rupee10 lakh book, and 0.0015 basis points of a \rupee100 crore
one. Any conclusion about a diversified strategy is therefore partly a statement about
assumed capital. We report this dependence explicitly rather than hiding it in a default.

\section{The auditor}
\label{sec:auditor}

\subsection{$A_{\mathrm{static}}$: leakage by structure, not by syntax}

The static layer parses candidate source with Python's \texttt{ast} module and classifies
findings by leak class, line number, and severity. Its two strongest rules are structural
rather than syntactic: \emph{did the constructor accept bulk data?} and \emph{did the
decision function read stored state instead of the point-in-time view?} These mirror the
engine's own information boundary, which is why they catch cheats whose results look
plausible; pattern-matching \texttt{shift(-1)} catches only the cheats that announce
themselves.

Against the fixture suite the layer achieves recall $1.00$ (3/3 leaky) and precision
$1.00$ (0/30 honest flagged). \textbf{This number should be discounted and we say so in the
paper rather than the appendix.} With syntax-based detectors alone, recall was $1/3$; the
structural rules were added after observing that failure, and were then measured on the same
three fixtures that motivated them. That is closer to training accuracy than to a test. The
genuine out-of-sample evaluation is against generated code the detectors were never designed
around (\S\ref{sec:leakclasses}).

\subsection{$A_{\mathrm{stat}}$: deflation against an honest denominator}

We compute the Deflated Sharpe Ratio of \citet{bailey2014}, which converts an observed
Sharpe $\widehat{SR}$ into the probability that it exceeds what $N$ trials would produce by
luck, given sample length $T$, skewness $\gamma_3$ and kurtosis $\gamma_4$:
\[
\mathrm{DSR} \;=\; \Phi\!\left(
\frac{(\widehat{SR} - SR_0)\sqrt{T-1}}
     {\sqrt{1 - \gamma_3 \widehat{SR} + \frac{\gamma_4 - 1}{4}\widehat{SR}^{\,2}}}
\right),
\]
where $SR_0$ is the expected maximum Sharpe across $N$ trials---a function of both $N$ and
the \emph{dispersion} of the trials' Sharpes. The mechanism deserves a plain statement,
because it is routinely misread: deflation does not shrink the strategy's Sharpe, it raises
the bar. With widely dispersed trials, 200 attempts buy an expected best of $2.77$ by luck
alone, so an observed $2.0$ is \emph{worse} than chance would produce.

This is worthless without an honest $N$, and an LLM generator is a machine for losing count.
We therefore maintain a \textbf{hash-chained, append-only trial ledger} outside the
generator's write path. Every draw increments it---including syntax errors, runtime
failures, and retries. Verification walks the chain and raises on any broken link, naming
the edited entry. Nothing under \texttt{src/generate/} opens the file.

\begin{quote}\small\color{navy}
One deliberate imperfection: the ledger holds 1,877 entries but the true draw count is
1,887, because an early run recorded one entry per candidate rather than one per draw. We
did \textbf{not} retroactively edit the chain to insert the missing ten. An append-only
ledger that gets rewritten when found wrong is not tamper-evident. The correction is applied
at the point of use, and the discrepancy is deliberate.
\end{quote}

\subsection{$A_{\mathrm{sem}}$: does the story match the code?}

The semantic layer presents \texttt{qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4\_K\_M} with a candidate's stated
rationale and its implementation, and asks for one of four labels: \texttt{consistent},
\texttt{rationale\_implementation\_mismatch}, \texttt{unfalsifiable\_mechanism}, or
\texttt{unacknowledged\_known\_anomaly}. Validation is against 50 human-labelled items,
labelled before the model was run.

\textbf{Cohen's $\kappa = 0.589$} \citep{cohen1960}, raw agreement 38/50, at the top of the
``moderate'' band of \citet{landis1977}. Per-class agreement is uneven: \texttt{consistent}
26/31, \texttt{unfalsifiable\_mechanism} only 2/5. No prompt was tuned before this
measurement and none was tuned after it. The disposition---keep the layer---was decided
before the number was known.

One reconciliation is worth reporting because it is the ablation argument in miniature. The
human reviewer labelled \texttt{leak\_full\_sample\_scaler} as \texttt{consistent}, and that
is \emph{correct}: its rationale honestly describes what its code does, and its only defect
is where the scaler was fitted. It is caught by $A_{\mathrm{static}}$ and correctly passed by
$A_{\mathrm{sem}}$. \textbf{A leaky strategy can carry a perfectly honest rationale.} The
layers are not redundant, and any evaluation collapsing them into one ``flagged by
something'' signal would obscure that.

\section{Experimental design}
\label{sec:design}

\paragraph{Generation.} One model, one prompt (SHA256 digest
\texttt{f307433c\ldots}), temperature 0.8, candidate $i$ seeded at $42+i$. 1,550 draws in two
batches, pooled only after asserting identical model tag, prompt digest, seed rule and
window. Each candidate emits a rationale, a feature specification, entry and exit rules, and
executable code conforming to the engine's strategy interface. Every attempt increments the
trial ledger.

\paragraph{Evaluation.} Each candidate runs in a sandboxed worker with a 180-second bound
over the development window, net of costs, at a \rupee10 lakh book.

\paragraph{The abstention curve.} For coverage $c \in (0,1]$, rank candidates by auditor
confidence, retain the top $c$ fraction, and let $P(c)$ be the mean realised out-of-sample
Sharpe of the retained set. Then
\[
\mathrm{AUAP} \;=\; \int_0^1 P(c)\, \mathrm{d}c,
\]
evaluated on the grid $c \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2, \ldots, 1.0\}$. The baseline is rejection at
random at matched coverage, with a bootstrap 95\% interval. \textbf{An auditor that is
informative must produce $P(c)$ rising as $c \to 0$.}

\paragraph{Two design choices that shape the result, stated plainly.} Flat candidates---those
that executed but never traded---are excluded from the ranking population rather than ranked
last. They are all tied at exactly zero and at 64\% of the executed set would dominate every
retained set. Ranking them last would have been equally defensible and would have produced a
different, lower curve. Second, survivors handed forward to future work are defined by
$A_{\mathrm{static}} \wedge A_{\mathrm{sem}}$, excluding the statistical layer, which rejects
the entire corpus and would make the handoff empty by construction. \textbf{This is the one
place we chose the definition that produces a non-empty answer, and we flag it as such.}

\paragraph{Holdout discipline.} The 2025 calendar year was frozen before any holdout data was
fetched, set to a calendar boundary rather than to the latest available session so that
window length could not become a researcher degree of freedom. It has been evaluated exactly
twice, both under written authorisation logged with timestamps: once on a gross-of-costs
pipeline, and once after that pipeline was found defective and rebuilt. The first evaluation
is retired and its artifacts are frozen under a directory whose README forbids quoting them
without the word ``gross''. \textbf{The holdout is now permanently closed. No third
evaluation is available to this project under any circumstance.}

\section{Results}

\subsection{The corpus: strategies fail as programs, not as research}
\label{sec:corpus}

Read the first row of Table~\ref{tab:funnel} carefully. \emph{Every} draw returned
syntactically valid Python defining a conforming \texttt{Strategy} subclass with a rationale.
The model is entirely reliable at producing code that looks right. The attrition happens on
contact with data.

The 894 runtime errors are dominated by a single confusion: \texttt{AttributeError} (327)
and \texttt{ColumnNotFoundError} (247) together account for 64\% of failures, and both
trace overwhelmingly to the model calling pandas methods on polars frames, or treating a
wide frame as though it were long. The prompt states the distinction explicitly and carries
a worked example. The model makes the error anyway, in roughly a fifth of all draws.

\keyfinding{This relocates RQ1. The question ``what fraction of LLM-generated strategies
fail static leakage screening'' presumes leakage is the binding constraint. On this corpus
it is not. The auditor was never reached for 59\% of the corpus, and for a further 26\% it
had nothing to deflate. \textbf{An auditor tuned to catch cheating is guarding a door most
candidates never reach.}}

\subsection{Leak classes, and one number we do not believe}
\label{sec:leakclasses}

Static analysis needs no execution, so it ran over all 1,550 sources: 222 rejected (14.3\%),
of which 195 carried one class, 26 two, and one three. By class:
\texttt{snooped\_parameter} 166, \texttt{future\_indexing} 58, \texttt{target\_in\_features}
23, \texttt{boundary\_crossing\_window} 2, \texttt{survivorship\_selection} 1, and
\texttt{full\_sample\_statistic} \textbf{0}.

\textbf{We do not believe the zero.} Full-sample statistics are the most common leak class in
hand-written quantitative code, and a corpus of 1,550 candidates containing none of it is not
plausible. The cause is almost certainly ours: to raise the executable rate from near zero,
the prompt advises working in plain Python rather than in long polars expression chains, and
\texttt{pl.col("x").mean()} over an unfiltered frame is precisely where this class arises.
The class distribution above therefore describes \emph{this prompt}, not LLM-generated
strategies in general, and the zero should be read as evidence that the instrument is blind
here---not as evidence the defect is absent.

The semantic layer labelled 90.1\% of the corpus \texttt{consistent}, rejecting 154 (9.9\%):
119 rationale--implementation mismatches, 21 unacknowledged known anomalies, 14 unfalsifiable
mechanisms. It is not a detector that fires indiscriminately. Whether the 154 are the
\emph{right} ones is not established on this population, since $\kappa$ was measured on a
different one.

Mean and median diverge by a factor of thirteen on cost drag (Table~\ref{tab:grossnet}). The
mean is dragged by a tail of candidates churning above $80\times$ annual turnover; the median
strategy loses 0.19 of Sharpe and 4.4\% a year. Both are reported because quoting either
alone misleads.

The best gross strategy is also the best net strategy---because it barely trades, at
$2.8\times$ annual turnover and 0.4\% annual cost. That is the finding in miniature:
\textbf{cost survival selects for low turnover, not for skill}, which is the same conclusion
\citet{novymarx2016} reach for US anomalies, arrived at here through a very different door.

\paragraph{Standard factors are not spared.} Running eleven textbook factors through the same
pipeline as a positive control, drag rises monotonically with turnover in ten of eleven rows.
Two factors flip from profitable to unprofitable. Momentum \citep{jegadeesh1993} survives at
a net Sharpe of 0.9656---but equal-weighting the same universe returns 0.9615. \textbf{Net of
Indian costs, the momentum premium on this universe is inside the noise.}

\paragraph{The eleventh row, and why the book size matters.} The one factor that breaks
monotonicity is inverse-volatility weighting: the \emph{lowest} turnover of any active
strategy (0.011 per session) and the second \emph{highest} cost (10.18 bps per session). It
holds a hundred names and nudges each daily, and the depository charge is levied per scrip
regardless of size. Sweeping book size confirms the mechanism precisely:

The depository contribution falls $169\times$ across a $100\times$ book sweep, close to the
inverse proportionality the model predicts, and \textbf{the strategy's verdict reverses with
assumed capital}: $-1.38$ at \rupee10 lakh, $+0.69$ at \rupee10 crore. Momentum runs the
opposite way---its cost \emph{rises} with book size as impact and slippage grow while the

books by market impact.

We report the corpus at \rupee10 lakh, which was the pre-existing default, and we did not
change it after observing that it flips a sign. Changing a benchmark default in response to
an inconvenient result is the pathology this paper exists to detect.

\paragraph{How much of the drag is our assumption?} For a realistically-trading strategy,
79.6\% of the drag is published statutory rate, 3.9\% depository charge, and only 16.5\%
comes from the two coefficients we chose. \textbf{The headline is not hostage to our
slippage and impact defaults.} For the near-zero-turnover market reference the proportions
invert, but the absolute cost there is 0.1 bps and immaterial.

\subsection{Deflation at an honest trial count}
\label{sec:deflation}

At $N = 1{,}887$, the deflated Sharpe probability exceeds 0.95 for \textbf{0 of 631}
evaluated candidates. The corpus maximum is $4.4 \times 10^{-8}$. The best raw Sharpe in the
corpus, 1.3049, carries a deflated probability of $5.9\times10^{-5}$.

Those two numbers should feel violently inconsistent, and the inconsistency is the point:
apparent skill and statistical significance are different quantities, and the gap between
them is exactly what a trial count of 1,887 buys.

\textbf{We cannot rule out that the test is saturated.} The deflation term grows with $N$
while sample length is fixed at 1,232 sessions; at some ratio the test rejects everything
regardless of merit. That 631/631 are rejected is consistent with an honest correction
\emph{and} with a saturated one, and this data alone cannot separate them. We state this as
an open weakness rather than as a robustness claim.

The Probability of Backtest Overfitting could not be computed on the pooled corpus: the
eleven ruined strategies produce short series and the common-length matrix loses too many
columns. \textbf{We report it as unavailable rather than repairing it by dropping the ruined
strategies}, which would be a post-hoc restriction not part of the intended protocol. On the
twelve-fixture suite, where it can be computed, PBO is 0.588 (0.531--0.667 across split
counts)---above 0.5 in every variant, meaning selecting the in-sample best yields a
below-median strategy out of sample more often than not.

\textbf{No configuration beats random rejection at matched coverage} (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
Five sit inside the random interval; two sit below it, meaning they order strategies
\emph{worse} than chance. The null is not rejected.

One diagnostic is worth the space. On development data, the \texttt{statistical} layer alone
was the only configuration to beat random---and it provably cannot be evidence, because its
confidence is $1 - \mathrm{DSR}$ and DSR is a monotone function of the very development
Sharpe that $P(c)$ measures. Ranking by a quantity and then scoring on that same quantity
beats random by construction. Out of sample the comparison becomes legitimate, and the layer
fails with the rest, landing second-worst of seven. \textbf{The circularity diagnosis was
made by argument in advance and confirmed by experiment afterwards.}

The corpus also collapses wholesale from development to holdout: mean Sharpe $+{\sim}0.40$
gross in sample against $-1.0351$ net out of sample, with 216/221 positive in sample and
roughly half positive out of it. The best out-of-sample performer, at $+1.3618$, had a
development Sharpe of $-1.6696$. \textbf{The single best out-of-sample strategy is one any
sane process would have discarded in sample.} That is not a finding about the strategy. It is
multiple testing appearing inside our own results, which is the most direct evidence we can
offer that the phenomenon we set out to measure is real.

\section{Negative results}
\label{sec:negative}

This section exists because the charter governing this work requires it, and because a paper
that reports only what worked is the artifact this paper is about.

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.35em}
\item \textbf{$\mathrm{AUAP}$ does not beat random rejection}, in seven of seven
  configurations, out of sample, under a pre-registered protocol. Two configurations are
  worse than chance.
\item \textbf{The null survived a full correction of our own pipeline.} The first evaluation
  was gross of costs because no cost model existed. We built one, wired it in, re-ran
  everything downstream, and spent the second and final holdout authorisation. The
  magnitudes moved substantially---holdout mean from $-0.2755$ to $-1.0351$---and the
  conclusion did not move at all. We consider this the most informative single fact in the
  paper, because it distinguishes ``we found a null'' from ``we found a null and then checked
  whether it was an artifact of our own defect.''
\item \textbf{This null is confounded with corpus degeneracy}, and we cannot resolve the
  confound. The 225 rankable strategies are tightly clustered and mostly weak. A null
  therefore cannot distinguish \emph{``the auditor is uninformative''} from \emph{``this
  corpus is too weak to test the auditor''}. Both explanations survive the data. This caveat
  travels with the number permanently, and it is the reason the benchmark is released.
\item \textbf{The tracking-error decomposition failed.} Total tracking error against the
  index is 0.0775 over 1,231 sessions, but the two legs of the attempted decomposition came
  out at 0.1152 and 0.1463---each exceeding the total they were meant to partition. The
  traded-value proxy does not stand in for capitalisation weighting. Only the total is
  reported. Nothing was adjusted to make the numbers add up.
\item \textbf{One layer showed a suggestive signal that we decline to build on.} The semantic
  layer alone reaches $P(0.05) = -0.5147$ against a population mean of $-1.0351$: its most
  confident 5\% did roughly twice as well as average. Five per cent of 225 is eleven
  strategies, and the random interval's width says that is noise. It appears in the
  pre-specified ablation table and nowhere else in our argument.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A process failure we are obliged to report.} During the cost re-run, six
candidates were silently reclassified as runtime errors by a type-inference defect: summing
over an empty book returns integer zero, which caused a column to be inferred as integer and
raise on the first genuine float. The exit code was zero. Among the six was the candidate
holding the highest holdout Sharpe in the previous evaluation. It was caught only by
comparing funnel counts against the retired gross run---a check imposed by review, not by the
code. \textbf{Without that comparison this paper would have been wrong and would have looked
fine.} We report it because a paper about detecting silent failure that concealed its own
would be self-refuting.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{enumerate}\setlength\itemsep{0.3em}
\item \textbf{One generator, one prompt, one temperature.} Every corpus statistic describes
  \texttt{qwen2.5:7b-instruct-q4\_K\_M} at temperature 0.8 under a single prompt whose digest
  is published. These are not claims about LLM strategy generation in general. A stronger
  generator would plausibly change the funnel and might change the null.
\item \textbf{The prompt steers implementation style}, which biases the leak-class
  distribution downward in at least one class (\S\ref{sec:leakclasses}).
\item \textbf{The static layer's perfect fixture score is provisional}, for the reason given
  in \S\ref{sec:auditor}: its decisive rules were written after seeing it fail.
\item \textbf{Slippage and impact coefficients are uncalibrated.} They account for roughly
  16\% of drag on realistically-trading strategies at a \rupee10 lakh book, but they dominate
  in the large-book regime, so Table~\ref{tab:dp}'s right-hand rows are more assumption-laden
  than its left-hand ones.
\item \textbf{Three historical statutory rates could not be sourced} and are held constant
  across the window; each is documented with the maximum error it could introduce, jointly
  under one part in a thousand of a round trip. Stamp duty before July 2020 was
  state-dependent and the uniform rate overstates cost in the first six months.
\item \textbf{The universe is a liquidity proxy, not the NIFTY~100}, because free
  point-in-time index membership could not be obtained. It is survivorship-free by
  construction, which was the binding requirement, but it is not the index and is never
  described as such.
\item \textbf{Deflation may be saturated at $N = 1{,}887$} over 1,232 sessions
  (\S\ref{sec:deflation}).
\item \textbf{PBO is unavailable} on the pooled corpus, for the reason in
  \S\ref{sec:deflation}.
\item \textbf{Flat candidates are excluded from the ranking population} rather than ranked
  last; the alternative is defensible and would lower the curve.
\item \textbf{The holdout is a single calendar year} (246 sessions) of one market. Out-of-sample
  evidence of this length supports a direction, not a magnitude.
\end{enumerate}

% =====================================================================================
\section{Release}
\label{sec:release}

We release the fixture suites---three leaky strategies with documented cheats and thirty
honest ones---the audited 1,550-candidate corpus with per-layer verdicts, the 174 survivors
under the $A_{\mathrm{static}} \wedge A_{\mathrm{sem}}$ definition, the Indian transaction
cost model with its verification test, and the evaluation harness.

The benchmark protocol is deliberately restrictive: a fixed shared holdout, \textbf{one
evaluation per submission}, no tuning on holdout results, and no revision of the benchmark in
response to submissions. Independent one-shot evaluations do not contaminate a frozen test
set; iterative optimisation against it does, and a benchmark that permits the latter measures
nothing after its first few users.

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to determine whether an adversarial auditor can tell real trading signals from
artifacts in a corpus written by a language model. On the evidence assembled here, for this
generator, it cannot---not better than chance.

Three things were learned along the way that we did not expect to be the interesting part.
The dominant failure mode of an LLM strategy researcher is not dishonesty but inertness:
six candidates in ten never survive contact with data, and two thirds of the rest never
trade.
Indian transaction costs, honestly modelled, reverse the sign of one profitable strategy in
six and reduce the momentum premium on a liquid universe to the width of the noise. And an
honest trial count of 1,887 removes every trace of statistical significance from a corpus
whose best member has a superficially respectable Sharpe of 1.28.

The instrument works. It reported no signal because, on this evidence, there was none to
report---and it reported the same thing after we corrected our own most serious
implementation error, which is the closest thing to a control that a null result admits. We
release the apparatus so that a stronger generator can be pointed at it, and so that our
second claim can be contested by someone who disagrees with it.
