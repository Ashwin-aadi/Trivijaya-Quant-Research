## 1. What FlowState measures, and what it deliberately does not

FlowState reports **constraint-based deployment capacity**: the largest AUM at which a strategy can
place its trades without exceeding 1.0% of a session's traded value. It is
arithmetic on observed turnover and assumes nothing.

It does **not** report impact-erosion capacity — the AUM at which a strategy's own trading moves
prices enough to erase its edge. That requires an impact function, and §4 is the measurement showing
daily bars cannot supply one. The two answer different questions and only one of them is supported
by the data.

## 2. RQ1 — deployment capacity

Capacity is the **minimum across every session the strategy trades**, including the session that
builds its opening position. A strategy must be executable on all of its rebalances, not on a
typical one.

| Factor | Binding | Entry | Median session | Median ÷ binding | Sessions | One name | Relaxed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| momentum_12_1 | ₹6.94 cr | ₹28.61 cr | ₹31.4 cr | 4.5× | 887 | 27.4% | 1.54× |
| reversal_1m | ₹0.81 cr | ₹9.89 cr | ₹21.2 cr | 26.2× | 1,212 | 7.7% | 1.25× |
| low_volatility | ₹6.78 cr | ₹8.31 cr | ₹32.9 cr | 4.8× | 661 | 40.1% | 1.85× |
| illiquidity | ₹7.22 cr | ₹10.75 cr | ₹32.0 cr | 4.4× | 377 | 24.1% | 1.66× |
| liquidity_size_proxy | ₹7.91 cr | ₹16.02 cr | ₹33.9 cr | 4.3× | 383 | 18.8% | 1.56× |

**Range: ₹0.81 cr to ₹7.91 cr** — roughly one to ten million US
dollars, which is small.

**Why it is small, and why the least-liquid position decides it.** These books are equal-weighted
across each leg, so every name carries the same weight and must be traded in the same rupee size. A
strategy holding a ₹50 crore-a-day name alongside a ₹500 crore-a-day name trades both identically,
and the participation constraint binds on the smaller one first. Capacity is therefore set by the
least liquid position a strategy holds, not by the average liquidity of its book. The *one name*
column reports how often the tightest name is more than twice as tight as the next.

*Relaxed* is a robustness check, never an alternative definition: what capacity would be if the
single tightest position were dropped each session. It rises by well under an order of magnitude,
which is what establishes that these figures describe the strategy rather than one holding.

## 4. RQ3 — why daily OHLCV cannot price transient impact

Reported as a **principal finding**, not a limitation.

**The headline is non-identification.** Regressing the following week's return on today's, over the
heaviest-volume decile of sessions, gives an estimate whose *sign depends on the weighting scheme*:

| Horizon | Precision-weighted β | Equal-weighted β | Detectable, pooled | Detectable, one symbol |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | -0.0180 | -0.0114 | 0.0135 | 0.182 |
| 5 | -0.0395 | +0.0205 | 0.0267 | 0.368 |
| 10 | -0.0588 | +0.0370 | 0.0350 | 0.497 |
| 21 | -0.0996 | +0.0867 | 0.0477 | 0.699 |

n = 177 symbols. The two weightings **disagree on sign at
3 of 4 horizons.** Both are standard, neither is wrong, and
nothing in the data adjudicates between them.

Three further measurements agree:

- **Resolution.** Temporary impact decays over minutes to hours. The finest horizon daily bars
  permit is one session, already coarser than the phenomenon. The pooled reversal also *grows*
  monotonically with horizon rather than decaying, which is the signature of slow mean reversion
  rather than of a liquidity concession being repaid.
- **Exponent.** Fitting |return| against relative traded value gives a median exponent of
  0.689 against the square-root law's 0.5, with a 10th-to-90th percentile range of
  0.521 to 0.859 across 179 symbols, explaining a median
  0.126 of variance. Across a sample split the per-symbol exponent correlates
  0.374 with itself (n = 166).
- **Extrapolation.** A capacity study asks about 1.0% participation. Observed
  relative traded value has a median of 0.821 —
  1.91 orders of magnitude away — and only
  **2 of 208,042** symbol-days fall inside the region of
  interest.

**The control.** Amihud illiquidity, computed on the same data, correlates 0.836
across the same sample split (n = 166, p = 1.7e-44). The data supports a
stable liquidity *measure* while refusing to support an impact *model parameter*. **The failure is
in the model, not the data.**

## 5. Flow-conditional capacity

Deployment capacity computed separately by flow state, across 5 factors:

**Outflow ÷ inflow capacity ranges from 0.94× to 1.24×**, median
0.96×, on 76 to 234 sessions per cell.

**The hypothesis is not supported.** P3 predicted deployable size might collapse by an order of
magnitude when foreign participation turns. It does not move materially, and some ratios point the
wrong way.

**This null rests on a proxy and cannot distinguish two explanations.** No free source serves
historical daily FII/DII *cash-market* net flow; the series used is FII *equity-derivatives*
activity, denominated in contracts rather than rupees, over a window in which lot sizes changed, and
FII derivatives activity is substantially hedging against cash positions so its sign need not track
cash-market demand. Whether capacity is genuinely stable across flow states, or the proxy is simply
uninformative about cash liquidity, is not separable with this data.

## 6. The validation gap — a methodological finding

FlowState was validated on 5 standard factor strategies and then applied to
156 machine-generated strategies. Measured on the same statistic, the validation set spans
**9.8×** in capacity and the application population spans **30.9×** — a
ratio of 3.2×.

**Two defects lived in that gap, and both were found by the corpus rather than by the validation
set.** Floating-point residue was being counted as trading, and the headline statistic was
discarding the binding constraint. Both are documented in `CORRECTIONS.md`; the corrections moved
every published capacity figure **down**, by between 4.3× and 26×.

The lesson is general: **a benchmark's soundness cannot be established on the population that
motivated it.**

### The reference corpus

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Strategies with a deployment capacity | 156 |
| Strategies with a fragility score | 125 |
| **Carrying all three benchmark results** | **125** |
| Excluded as knife-edge by RegimeStress | 31 |

Corpus binding capacity: median ₹2.60 cr, 5th–95th percentile
₹0.35 to ₹10.8 cr, maximum
₹66.4 cr. Using the median session instead of the binding session would have
overstated the typical corpus strategy by 10.6×. Entry is the binding
session for 32 of 156 strategies (20.5%), so the
median was hiding more than the entry constraint alone.
